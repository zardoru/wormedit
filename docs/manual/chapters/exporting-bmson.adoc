[#exporting-bmson]
= Exporting a BMSON chart to other formats

BMSON is the one chart format that stores _stems_: long audio files that a chart cuts into
pieces with slice points. osu!mania, StepMania and BMS have no such thing. Their charts name
whole sound files and play them from a note. Wormedit bridges the two by cutting the stems for
you when you export.

This chapter covers the main use, converting a BMSON chart to another format, and a secondary
one: handing a BMSON chart to a player that cannot read BMSON. For the export screen itself,
see <<walkthrough-bms>> and <<walkthrough-osu-stepmania>>; this chapter only adds what is
specific to BMSON.

== The workflow

. Open the `.bmson` file, or drop it on the window (<<opening>>). Its audio files must be next
to it and decodable; sounds that are missing are reported as unresolved resources.
. Press kbd:[Ctrl+S] and save the `.wormedit` project. Exporting needs a saved project.
. On *Project* (kbd:[F1]), under *Exports*, add or edit a target: osu!mania, StepMania or BMS.
If the project has no target, choose a suggestion in the empty *Exports* section. Include the
BMSON chart in the target.
. Open the target's *⋯* menu and choose *Preview*. It lists the files that would be written,
including the generated sound files, and every warning, without writing anything.
. Click *Export*. Wormedit publishes the chart and its generated sounds together. If anything
fails, nothing is written.

Exporting to a native `.wormedit` project or back to BMSON keeps everything described below
as it is. Stems, slice points and channels survive, and nothing is cut.

== What a BMSON file becomes in Wormedit

Wormedit sorts each BMSON sound channel into one of two kinds when it imports the chart:

Stem::
A channel with at least one continuation note (`c: true`). Its notes are slice points in one
long audio file. A stem gets its own BGM lane in Compose, named after the file.
Keysound::
A channel with only restart notes. Every note plays its file from the start. The BGM events of
all keysound channels are packed into the fewest BGM columns that avoid overlaps, named
*BGM 1*, *BGM 2* and so on.

Playable notes keep their sound and their link to the channel event they came from. If a
playable note and a BGM note play the same slice at the same pulse, the BMSON specification says
to drop the BGM note, and the importer does. Moving a linked playable note to BGM
(kbd:[`]) puts it back on its stem, with its timing and slice point.

[#bmson-authoring-sounds]
== Authoring BMSON sounds: keysounds and stems

Everything in <<exporting-bmson>> about stems and keysounds also applies when you build a BMSON
chart from scratch. Each sound in a BMSON chart is one of two kinds, and you choose the kind
when you add the sound (and can change it later).

=== What "stem" means here

In Wormedit, _stem_ is a name for how a sound behaves, not for what the audio is. A stem is any
sound whose file is cut into slices or played on with continue markers (`c: true`). It can be
a full-mix stem such as drums or vocals, but it can just as well be a loop you have to chop, a
sample you play in sections, or a whole song cut into parts. If you will cut it or continue it,
make it a stem, whatever the audio is.

The reverse also holds: a sound that is only ever started from its beginning is a keysound,
even if it is long or came from a stem-like source.

=== The problem keysounds solve

A stem is built for one long audio file that you cut into slices. Every stem gets its own BGM
lane, and its events are slice points (`c: true` or `c: false`) on that lane.

Many sounds are not like that. An impact, a crash, a vocal chop or a drum hit is a one-shot: it
plays from its start every time and will never have a continue marker. Giving each of them its
own stem lane would fill the chart with lanes that do nothing a stem is for, and for the export
it would mean slice bookkeeping for a sound that is never sliced.

A *keysound* (also called a packed sound) is the kind for these sounds. Its events carry no
slice points, and Wormedit packs the events of all keysounds into the fewest shared BGM columns
(*BGM 1*, *BGM 2*...), so a hundred different one-shots take a few columns, not a hundred
lanes. Because a keysound is never cut, it exports as exactly one file however many times you
use it.

Use a stem only when the sound will have `c: true` markers, that is, when you will cut one
file and play it on from slice to slice, whatever kind of audio it is.

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| |Keysound (packed) |Stem
|What it is |A short sound, used as a whole. Every note that plays it starts it from the beginning.
|One long audio file that the chart cuts into slices at its marker points.
|Where its BGM events live |Packed into the fewest *BGM 1*, *BGM 2*... columns that avoid overlaps. |Its own BGM lane, named after the file.
|Markers |Restart only. |Restart (`c: false`) or continue (`c: true`).
|Added from |*Add keysound...* in the Sounds dock. |*Add stem...* in the Sounds dock.
|===

=== Adding and placing keysounds

. Open the Sounds dock and press *Add keysound...* in its Keysounds section. Choose the
files. The sounds are registered and no notes are placed.
. Arm a sound (double-click its row, or kbd:[Ctrl+K]) and place notes with it, on playable
lanes or on a BGM column, as described in <<arming-sounds>>.
. Keysound events on BGM columns are packed automatically: you never create or name a packed
column yourself.

A packed event always plays its whole file. It has no slice points, so two events of the
same keysound produce one file when the chart is exported (see below).

=== Adding and cutting stems

. Press *Add stem...* in the Stems section. This creates the sound, an empty channel and a stem
lane, and places nothing.
. Select the stem lane and cut it. *Insert continue at cursor* and *Insert restart at cursor*
place a marker at the cursor, and *Razor continue* and *Razor restart* cut by clicking the
lane. A _continue_ marker (`c: true`) plays on from where the previous slice stopped. A
_restart_ marker (`c: false`) plays the file again from its start.
. Playable notes you link to a slice keep their sound. kbd:[`] returns a linked playable note
to its stem.

=== Changing a sound between stem and keysound

Right-click a stem lane header and choose *Pack into columns*, right-click a packed event
and choose *Make stem*, or use the *Kind* button in the Sounds tab's Properties column. Each
is one undo step, and a round trip leaves the chart unchanged.

A stem that has any continue marker cannot be packed: the reason shown is
"continuation points need a stem lane". A packed channel plays its whole file at every event,
so continue markers have no meaning there. If you want to pack it anyway, choose
*Convert continue points to restarts and pack*. This changes the audio: every marker now
restarts the file from the beginning, instead of continuing where the previous slice
stopped. It is meant for a stem that really was a single sound hit many times, not for a
long track cut into parts. Undo restores the stem lane and its markers in one step.

=== What this means for export

* A keysound (packed) channel is never cut. Every event plays the whole file, and the file is
shared by all events and, when you export several charts together, by all charts.
* Each distinct slice of a stem becomes its own WAV file. Two slices share a file only when
they cut exactly the same range of the same source. Repeats of the same rhythm share files,
but a different tempo or different spacing before the next marker gives a different file.
* Sharing works across the charts of a project. When you export several charts of one project
together, a slice that two charts cut identically (the same source file, the same first and
last frame) is rendered once, and both charts use that one file. It counts once against the BMS
limit. Charts that cut the same stem differently, for example at another tempo, get separate
files. Charts exported on their own, one export at a time, share nothing.
* The number of exported files, and for BMS the sound limit of 1295, therefore depends on how
many different slices you cut. A chart that reuses one sound many times as a keysound costs one
sound. Cutting the same file as a stem into many slices costs one per slice.
* For StepMania and osu!mania, slice files are not limited in number but are written as float32
WAV, so a heavily sliced chart makes a large folder. *Preview* lists the sounds and losses
before you export.

[TIP]
====
Rule of thumb: if a sound will never have a continue marker, make it a keysound. If a stem
turns out to be only ever restarted, pack it: it exports as one file instead of one per
slice, and stops taking a lane.
====

=== Known limitation: BMS slots are counted per export, not per chart

When you export several charts of one project to BMS together, the sound limit of 1295 is
counted over the union of every sound any of those charts uses. Each BMS file then declares all
of them, with the same slot numbers, even sounds that its own chart never plays. A sound that
only chart A uses still takes a slot in chart B's file.

For example, chart A cuts beats 1-4 of a stem and chart B cuts beats 1-2 of the same stem and
never 1-4. These are two different slices, so they are two files. Exported together, both
files declare both slices. Exported one at a time, each file declares only its own.

Sounds are shared between the charts, as described above, but unused ones are not yet released
from the slot count. If a project is close to the limit, export the charts one at a time, and
use *Preview* to check the count first. This is planned to change: sounds will stay shared
while the slots an individual chart does not use are left free.

== What changes in other formats

osu!mania, StepMania and BMS cannot play part of a file. For these targets, Wormedit:

. finds every distinct slice of every stem, from the slice's source file and its first and last
frame;
. renders one WAV file for each distinct slice, named `<source name>_001.wav`, `_002` and so on,
and avoids names already used in the project. Slices that repeat share one file;
. binds each playable note and each BGM event to the file for its slice.

The generated files are stereo, 44.1{nbsp}kHz, 32-bit float WAV, so the converted chart plays the
same samples as the original. They are written next to the chart by the same all-or-nothing
publication as the chart itself. A continuation that starts at or after the end of its file has
no audio; the note stays in the chart without a sound.

Keysound channels are not cut. Each one becomes one sound used by every note that plays it.

[cols="1,2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| |osu!mania |StepMania |BMS

|Playable notes
|Hit sample on the note.
|Keysound index on the note. The files are listed in `#KEYSOUNDS`, which has no size limit.
|`#WAV` slot on the note.

|BGM events
|Storyboard `Sample` events. A hold tail on a BGM event is dropped, with a warning.
|Autokeysound cells in a free column at that row. If none is free, the event is dropped and a
warning counts them.
|BGM channels, one per packed column or stem lane. A hold tail is dropped, with a warning.

|Scratch lanes
|Ordinary key columns.
|Ordinary columns.
|Kept as BMS scratch, if the layout has a compatible BMS channel profile.

|Sound limit
|None.
|None.
|1295 sounds.
|===

=== BMS and the sound limit

BMS names sounds with two-character base-36 numbers, so a file can hold 1295 sounds. A BMSON
chart converts to BMS only if the sounds fit: each distinct slice, each other sound the chart
still uses, and one shared silent sample for notes that have no audio. If they do not fit,
the export is refused before anything is written, with a message such as "needs 1400 sounds,
BMS holds 1295". Run *Preview* to find out before you export. Sounds that nothing references
do not count. A BMSON layout also needs a compatible BMS channel profile.

=== osu!mania timing precision

osu!mania stable files store autoplay sample times in whole milliseconds. Wormedit rounds them
and warns with the largest error it introduced, which is never more than half a millisecond.
The lazer profile (*lazer-v128*) keeps fractional times and gives no warning.

== Timing

BMSON counts in pulses and has no measures or time signatures. On import, Wormedit maps pulses
to exact divisions of a measure, and the `lines` field defines where the measures start and so
how long they are. A file with no `lines` gets 4/4 measures. Timing then goes through each
target's own rules:

* *BMS* and *StepMania* keep BPM changes and stops.
* *osu!mania* accepts time signatures with a denominator of 4 and a numerator from 1 to 32. It
cannot store pauses, warps, speed curves or sections, so a chart that uses them is refused
instead of exported with the timing changed.

== What does not carry over

* Stems become separate files. The slice points no longer exist in the output, only the files
they produced. Re-importing the converted chart gives keysounds, not stems.
* Hold tails on BGM events are dropped in osu!mania and BMS.
* Anything else the target cannot store is reported as a loss in *Preview*, and the target's
loss policy decides whether the export is refused.

== Legacy players

BMSON is the format to author in. Players that cannot read it are legacy targets, and the
sliced exports exist to serve them. osu!mania and Quaver are the exception: they are games with
their own audience and charting conventions, not legacy players, so converting to them is a
use case of its own and not a compatibility copy. Keep the `.wormedit` project as the source and re-export
after each edit instead of editing the converted files. Because StepMania has no sound limit,
it is the easiest legacy copy to keep complete. BMS needs the sound count to stay under 1295,
so run *Preview* after adding slices.

== Check the result

. Read every warning in *Preview*; losses are listed there.
. Open the exported chart in Wormedit and use <<comparing,Compare / Validate>> to compare it with
the original difficulty.
. Play both in Compose. The sliced WAVs should sound the same as the stems.
