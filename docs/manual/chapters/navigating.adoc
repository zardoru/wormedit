= Moving around a chart

.Compose in a new BME project: timing lanes on the left, eight playable lanes and the Sounds dock (captured at {shot-compose-overview-revision})
image::compose-overview.png[Compose showing BMS measure labels, timing lanes, eight playable lanes, BGM column controls and the Sounds dock]

The yellow horizontal line is the *edit cursor*. Keyboard navigation moves the chart under it; the
line itself stays at the same place on screen. Lane keys, keyboard holds and range selections all
act at the cursor.

The arrow keys move in the direction you see on screen, so kbd:[Up] means "up the screen" whether
the chart scrolls up or down (the scroll direction button in the toolbar switches between the two).

[#chart-rows]
== Rows, beats and snap

A *row* is one exact musical position across the chart's lanes. Notes on the same row
start together. Row numbers count the chart's smallest representable steps along the
musical timeline; they do not count screen pixels or a fixed number of milliseconds.
Tempo changes, stops and other timing events determine when those positions play.

The chart's *resolution* sets how many row steps fit in one standard four-quarter-note
measure. A common resolution is *960*: a quarter-note beat spans 240 rows, so the four
beats of a standard measure begin at rows *0, 240, 480 and 720*. The next measure begins
after 960 steps. Imported charts can use another resolution; 960 is not a universal constant.
A shorter or longer measure contains proportionally fewer or more rows.

*Snap* chooses which of those positions edits land on. At resolution 960, a standard-measure
`1/16` snap steps by 60 rows. Turning snap off allows individual row steps; it does not make
the timeline infinitely fine. Zoom changes the spacing on screen without changing the rows.

Scripts distinguish *local rows*, counted from 0 at the start of a measure, from *absolute
rows*, counted from 0 at the start of the chart. A position's absolute row is its measure's
starting row plus its local row. For example, local row 240 in a measure starting at absolute
row 960 is absolute row 1200. In a 960-row measure, local rows run from 0 through 959;
its ending boundary is the next measure's row 0. See the
link:lua-api/index.html[Lua API reference] for the fields that expose these positions.

== Snaps and the cursor

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Keys |Movement

|kbd:[Up] / kbd:[Down]
|One snap. Hold the key to keep moving.

|kbd:[Left] / kbd:[Right]
|Coarser or finer snap. The list skips snaps the chart's resolution cannot represent exactly;
the current snap shows in the status bar (for example `1/4`).

|kbd:[PgUp] / kbd:[PgDn] (RePág / AvPág; macOS: kbd:[Option+Up] / kbd:[Option+Down])
|Skip one measure, and at least two beats. Holding repeats after a short delay.

|kbd:[Tab] held, then kbd:[Up] / kbd:[Down] or kbd:[Left] / kbd:[Right]
|Tab accelerates: each press moves a whole measure instead of a snap, repeating while held.

|kbd:[Tab] held, then kbd:[PgUp] / kbd:[PgDn]
|Two measures (at least four beats) per press.

|kbd:[Ctrl+G]
|Go to a measure by number.
|===

The *Quantize / Snap* section of the inspector shows every grid (1/4 to 1/80, and *custom…* for
any other division). The snap button in the toolbar turns snapping off, so mouse edits land on the
finest row instead.

== Bookmarks and sections

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Keys |Action

|kbd:[Ctrl+B]
|Add a bookmark at the cursor.

|kbd:[Ctrl+Up] / kbd:[Ctrl+Down]
|Previous or next bookmark.

|kbd:[Alt+Left] / kbd:[Alt+Right]
|Previous or next bookmark or section boundary.

|kbd:[Ctrl+Shift+G]
|Choose a bookmark by name.
|===

Open the *Markers* dock to add, rename, move or delete bookmarks and sections. It contains
beat-based and audio-time groups. Click a beat marker to navigate and double-click its label to
rename it. Expand *Find marker* for the searchable navigation list; Ctrl+Shift+G still opens the
bookmark chooser. Marker editing controls live in this dock.

Double-click a bookmark's line or label to rename it.

[#export-cuts]
== Audio cuts (export cuts)

Use export cuts to make a shorter version of a song across all its difficulties without
destructively editing the source audio or deleting the original chart content. This differs
from <<slicing-keysounds,slicing a sound into keysounds>>, which creates separate WAV resources.

Select an area in Compose and choose *Create export cut* from its context menu, beside
*Create section*. The selection uses the current snap setting. Cuts belong to the whole song,
so the same intervals apply to every chart. Open *Markers > Export cuts* to select a cut,
set its optional label and source-song start/end in milliseconds, then click *Apply cut*.
*Delete cut* removes the selected cut. Each change is one Undo step.

Ordinary Compose collapses cut intervals from its field and waveform; playback, seeking and
navigation skip them. Switch to *Timing focus* with kbd:[F3] to see the original timeline and
pink hatched cut bands, hear the original audio, and drag either edge. Release commits the
snapped edge; kbd:[Escape] cancels. Inverted or empty intervals are rejected.

Exports remove the union of overlapping or adjacent cuts once, splice the exported song audio,
and rebase every chart to the same clock. Separate cuts remain independently editable.
Objects entirely inside a cut are dropped with diagnostics. A hold, authored keysound/BGM or
BGA crossing a cut boundary blocks export; adjust the cut or the object first. Timing state
at the end of the cut carries into the retained part. The saved project and source media keep
their original content.

SM/SSC exports arrange the retained content into standard four-beat note-map bars without
moving its times. Export also refuses timing combinations that the target model cannot express
exactly, such as a required SPEED jump inside a stop or at a suppressed warp position; the
diagnostic identifies the timing event to adjust.

== Mouse wheel and zoom

* The wheel scrolls the chart without moving notes or snapping the cursor.
* kbd:[Alt] + wheel moves the cursor to the next snap, one step per burst of scrolling.
* kbd:[Ctrl] + wheel zooms around the pointer. The zoom buttons and *Reset view* are at the left
of the toolbar.

== Playback

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Keys |Action

|kbd:[Space]
|Play from the cursor; press again to stop.

|kbd:[Ctrl+Shift+Space]
|Play from the start of the chart.

|kbd:[Shift+Space]
|Play the selection in a loop.

|kbd:[Ctrl+F11] / kbd:[Ctrl+F12]
|Slower or faster playback.
|===

While the preview plays, the navigation keys move the playhead instead of the edit cursor, so you
can skip around without stopping.

[#keyboard-ranges]
== Selecting a range from the keyboard

* Hold kbd:[Shift] and move with kbd:[Up], kbd:[Down] or the page keys: the range grows from where
you started. Releasing kbd:[Shift] selects everything in it.
* kbd:[Ctrl+Space] starts a range that stays open without holding a key. Move the cursor (or
scroll), then press kbd:[Ctrl+Space] again to select it. kbd:[Space] starts or stops playback
without closing the range: its tail follows the playhead, including backward seeks, and stays
at the last position when playback stops or ends. Continue with cursor movement/scrolling,
or start playback again. During playback, kbd:[Escape] stops and keeps the range; press
kbd:[Escape] again while stopped to cancel it. Left-clicking the chart canvas outside the playable
lanes also cancels; transport buttons and clicks on the inspector background keep the range.
The status hint shows whether the range is latched or following playback.
