[#sounds-and-layers]
= Sounds and layers

Every note you place carries two choices made in advance: the *armed sound* and the *layer
target*. Arming them once and then placing notes from the keyboard is what keeps keysounded
charting quick.

[#arming-sounds]
== Arming a sound

The Sounds dock (kbd:[Ctrl+Shift+K] shows or hides it) lists the project's sounds with their BMS
ID, name, how many notes use them and their state.

* *Double-click* a row to arm it. The row is outlined and marked _armed_, and the Draw label names
it. A single click only selects the row; with preview-on-click turned on it also plays it.
* *kbd:[Ctrl+K]* (Go to sound) opens a search box: type part of a name and press kbd:[Enter] to
arm that sound without leaving the keyboard.
* In *BMS slots* mode the dock lists `#WAV` slots instead; clicking a filled slot arms its sound.

[#bms-slots]
== Importing into specific BMS slots

Some BMS workflows need a sound in an exact `#WAV` slot: matching an existing chart's numbering,
pasting notes from iBMSC or BMSE (which refer to sounds by slot number), or keeping a keysound set
in a fixed order. The Sounds dock's *BMS slots* mode does this. It is available for BMS charts
only.

. Click *BMS slots* at the top of the Sounds dock. The list shows the slots `01` to `ZZ` (1295 of
them, in base 36) and which sound each holds. The search box finds a slot or a sound.
. Click the slot where the import should start. An empty slot is fine. Choosing a slot also sets the
start for a multi-file import; with no chosen slot, new sounds use Automatic IDs.
. Click *+* (_Choose files for consecutive BMS WAV slots_) and pick the files, or drop them onto the
list:
** *One file* goes into the selected slot. If the slot already holds a sound, Wormedit asks
before replacing it (*Replace occupied WAV slot … with this file?*).
** *Several files* fill consecutive slots starting at the selected one. Wormedit shows the slot
planned for each file for review before importing, and refuses an import that would run past `ZZ`.

.BMS slots mode with the empty slot 09 selected as the import target (captured at {shot-bms-slots-revision})
image::bms-slots.png[The Sounds dock in a new BME project, listing empty WAV slots 01 to 0G with slot 09 selected]

Each import is one Undo step. Switch back with *Automatic IDs*, where new sounds take the next free
IDs.

The armed sound goes on every note you place: lane keys, keyboard holds, Draw clicks and drags,
and kbd:[Alt]-drag holds. A missing sound (its file was not found) cannot be armed.

The dock's filters (*All*, *Used*, *Unused*, *Missing*, the group and layer lists, and the search
box) narrow long lists, and the play button on each row previews a sound.

== Working with existing keysounds

* kbd:[Ctrl+Shift+A] selects every note in the chart that uses the selected note's sound.
* kbd:[Ctrl+Shift] + click a note selects the notes with its sound in the visible part of the
chart; kbd:[Ctrl+Alt] + click does it for the whole chart.
* *Play sound on click* (in the command menu, kbd:[Ctrl+Shift+P]) plays each note's sound when you
click it.

== Painting sounds on existing notes

Double-click a Sample in Compose's Sounds dock to arm it. Choose *Paint keysounds / osu!
hitsounds* (the audio-lines tool), or *Paint Hitsound / Keysound* from the command menu.
Drag across existing notes or holds, then release: the whole stroke is one Undo step.
Fast drags include notes crossed between pointer events; the brush never places or moves notes.
Locked, hidden and conditional notes are skipped.

When the osu! hitsound palette is shown, its controls arm the brush even when notes are selected.
The stroke applies those settings together with the armed sample. Without an armed sample,
it paints only hitsounds and keeps each note's catalog assignment. Escape cancels the current
stroke; press it with no active stroke to return to Select. Losing focus cancels the stroke.

== Sound tags

On the *Sounds* tab, select a sound to edit its properties. Type a tag and click *Add tag*
(or press kbd:[Enter]); click a tag chip to remove it. Each change is one Undo step and leaves
the sound's name, kind and asset unchanged. Duplicate tags are ignored without regard to case.
There can be up to 32 tags of 64 bytes each; rejected edits keep the existing tags and show a
diagnostic. *Filter by tag* matches part of an individual tag, without regard to case. The
ordinary search also finds names and tag text.

== Slicing a long sound

Save the project, select a difficulty, and select a sound on the *Sounds* tab.
Under the waveform, *Slice into keysounds* accepts *BPM*, *Division per beat*, and
*Offset (seconds)*. Division 4 makes four slices per beat (sixteenth notes).
The cut lines follow waveform zoom and pan; negative offsets are allowed, and a
positive offset keeps the leading audio as a separate slice.

Click *Slice* to render WAVs into the project's audio folder and register them as
keysounds in one Undo step. The source stays untouched, and no notes are placed
in Compose. The file-job status shows progress and offers *Cancel*. Cancellation,
a failed render/import, or changing the document while the job runs imports
nothing and removes the unaccepted output. Undo removes the imported catalog
entries while retaining their files for Redo. The preview refuses grids with
more than 1295 slices.

== osu! hitsounds

osu!mania charts show hitsound controls below the note-kind palette. The whistle, cymbal and
clapping-hands icons toggle Whistle, Finish and Clap. Their small checkboxes show off, on or a
mixed-selection dash; clicking a mixed addition turns it on for every selected note. Below
them, the music-note and layers selectors choose the normal and addition sample sets. They
show *A*, *N*, *S* or *D* for Auto, Normal, Soft or Drum, with a dropdown chevron. Click to cycle,
or right-click to choose a set.

With notes selected, a tile edits the selection in one Undo step. With nothing selected, it
sets defaults for the next notes you place, including painted notes and holds. These settings
do not replace BMS keysound assignments. You can also add hitsounds to a native project:
use *Show hitsound palette* in the command menu to enable the tiles, then select existing
notes to edit them, or clear the selection to set defaults for new notes. There is no need
to import an osu! chart first. Note settings are saved in `.wormedit` and used by playback
and preview-audio export. The same command enables the tiles for other chart formats.

The default shortcuts are kbd:[W]/kbd:[E]/kbd:[R] for additions,
kbd:[Shift+Q]/kbd:[Shift+W]/kbd:[Shift+E]/kbd:[Shift+R] for the normal set and the same letters
with kbd:[Alt] for the addition set. They are remappable in *Options → Input & shortcuts*.

Playback prefers map samples, then the folder set under *Options → Playback & audio → osu!
hitsound skin folder*, then Wormedit's bundled samples. Auto sets, index zero and volume zero
inherit the map's timing-point settings.

Notes with hitsounds show compact sample-set and addition icons, with contrast chosen for the
note color. Hover a note for the requested and resolved sets, additions, custom index, volume
and filename. Imported custom-set references are retained for playback; they are not a separate
custom-set authoring interface.

*Play sound on click* also auditions these hitsounds when you select or place a note. Independently,
*Playback → Play Hitsounds on Playback* turns hitsound monitoring on or off (on by default).
It does not mute music or ordinary BMS keysounds. Preview-audio export still includes the authored
mix, regardless of this live-monitoring setting.

[#song-preview]
== Song preview

The song preview is one start point, with an optional length, in the song's audio time. It
belongs to the song, so every difficulty shares it. Moving BPM changes or the offset leaves it
on the same audio. The *Song preview* group at the end of the Markers panel shows and edits it:

* Type a start and an end in milliseconds and click *Apply typed times*. Leave the end empty
  for a point-only preview.
* *Start at cursor* moves the start to the Compose cursor and keeps the length.
* *From selection* (or *Set song preview from selection* in the area menu) uses the selected
  area as the whole span.
* *Clear preview* removes it.

Compose draws the preview as a green band labelled _Preview_. A point-only preview shows a
fainter band for the 15 seconds wormedit plays and exports by default; that length is never
saved. Drag the start or end line to resize the span, or drag the _Preview_ label to move all of
it. Edges land on the grid while snapping is on. Dragging a point-only preview's start line
moves the point, and dragging its end line gives it a length. Each drag is one Undo step, and
Escape cancels it.

Each format keeps what it can store. osu! saves only the start (`PreviewTime`) and warns when a
length is dropped. StepMania saves the start and, when set, the length (`SAMPLESTART` and
`SAMPLELENGTH`). BMS and BMSON name a preview file instead, which you make with
*Export preview audio…*. An unchanged preview keeps its original spelling in the file, and the
raw format-field editor in Metadata refuses to change these fields.

When you import an osu! difficulty whose preview differs from the song's, that difficulty keeps
its own preview. The band then reads _Preview (this chart)_, and *Use song preview* returns it
to the song's.

== Exporting preview audio

Click *Export preview audio…* in the Song preview group to render the song preview. To render
another interval once, select a materialized area in Compose, right-click it and choose
*Export preview audio…*.
The dialog shows the resolved source interval. Choose an `.ogg` filename, optionally shorten
the duration (trimming the end), and set a fade-out. Relative filenames are next to the project;
the default is `preview.ogg`. A render can be at most ten minutes.

Enable *Replace an existing file* only when you want to replace that output. Project assets
cannot be overwritten. Click *Export* to mix chart autoplay in the background; the job bar
offers cancellation. The encoder's result is decoded and checked before publication. A failed,
cancelled or stale request leaves any existing output unchanged and never edits the chart.

[#layers]
== Layers

Layers are named, colored tags on notes, for example _drums_, _melody_ or _left hand_. They help
you find, hide and edit parts of a dense chart. The *Layer view*
button in the toolbar colors notes by layer.

Open the Layers dock with kbd:[Ctrl+L] and create a layer with kbd:[Ctrl+Shift+L]. Each layer can
be hidden, locked or soloed, and the *Require* and *Exclude* filters show only notes with (or
without) a layer.

.A target layer and the active Layer brush (captured at {shot-layers-dock-revision})
image::layers-dock.png[Melody selected in the Layers dock and Layer active in the toolbar]

=== Targeting a layer

Click a layer in the dock to make it the *target*, or press kbd:[Ctrl+1] to kbd:[Ctrl+9] for the
first nine layers in the dock. kbd:[Ctrl+0] clears the target.

What new notes get depends on the target:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Target |New notes get

|A layer
|That layer (a locked layer is skipped).

|*No layer*
|No layers at all, even if their sound has default layers.

|Nothing targeted
|Their sound's default layers.
|===

=== Default layers for a sound

Select one or more sounds in the Sounds dock and use its *Sound layers* box to add or remove
layers. Notes placed with those sounds afterwards start on those layers when nothing is targeted.

=== Painting layers onto notes

* The *Layer* tool (kbd:[Alt+3]): click or drag over notes to add them to the target layer;
kbd:[Alt]-drag removes them. The target must be visible and unlocked.
* With notes selected, the dock's *Add/remove selected notes* button changes them all at once.
