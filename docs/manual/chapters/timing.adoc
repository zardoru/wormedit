[#timing]
= Timing

Timing is everything that decides _when_ a row of the chart plays and _how fast_ it scrolls: tempo
changes, stops, scroll speed and the song's offset. In Wormedit, timing lives on the same canvas
as the notes, in the timing lanes at the left of Compose. Get it right before charting a long
song; the notes follow it.

== The timing lanes

[cols="1,2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Lane |Event |Used by

|BPM
|Tempo change. The chart's starting BPM sits on the first row.
|All formats.

|SV
|Scroll velocity: how fast the notes scroll, without changing the music.
|osu!mania (SV) and StepMania (`#SCROLLS`).

|SPD
|StepMania speed change, with a transition length.
|StepMania (`#SPEEDS`).

|Stop
|The chart pauses while the music keeps playing; notes at the row play before the pause.
|BMS (`#STOP`) and StepMania.

|Delay
|Like a stop, but notes at the row play after the pause.
|StepMania (`#DELAYS`).

|Warp
|Skips a span of the chart instantly.
|StepMania (`#WARPS`).

|Kiai
|osu!mania kiai time on or off.
|osu!mania.

|Anchor
|Pins a beat to an exact moment of the song audio; beat fitting pivots on anchors.
|Wormedit only.
|===

*Timing Settings* (in the command menu, kbd:[Ctrl+Shift+P]) chooses which timing lanes are shown.
Hiding a lane only hides it: its events keep working in playback and export.

[#editing-timing-events]
== Adding and editing timing events

* *Double-click* an event to edit it in place. Double-click an empty row of a lane to start a new
event there; it shows as a pending marker until you confirm it with kbd:[Enter] or *Confirm
event*, and kbd:[Escape] discards it.
* *Drag* a timing marker along its lane to move it to another snapped row.
* *BPM* is typed as a decimal, such as `174`, `174.5` or `174.375`, and stored exactly. Values
that came from an imported chart and have no exact decimal show a rounded decimal marked as
approximate; *Advanced exact fraction* shows and edits the exact value. Opening and closing the
editor never rounds the stored value.
* *Stops* and *delays* take a duration in seconds or, for BMS, in `#STOP` ticks. *SV* and *SPD*
take a multiplier; *SPD* also takes a transition length.
* In Timing focus (kbd:[F3]), kbd:[Ctrl]-click an empty BPM row to add a change with the current
tempo, and kbd:[Alt]-click the Anchor lane to add an anchor.
* *Insert BPM Change* and *Insert Scroll Velocity* in the command menu add an event at the cursor.
* Select events like notes and press kbd:[Delete] to remove them.

For BPM, stop, delay and warp edits, musical positions stay fixed by default.
*Keep audio time of notes* instead repositions playable notes, both hold ends,
BGM spans and retained sound-channel events to preserve their existing audio times.
Audio-time bookmarks and sections already store times and stay unchanged.
The edit preview reports how many objects move and how many positions need rounding.
Enable *Allow quantization* explicitly to accept that rounding; otherwise the edit
is rejected without changing the chart. New collisions and invalid lane layouts
still reject the whole edit. The chart grows when preserving time needs more measures.
This is an option for the current edit, and the entire edit is one Undo step.
Retained BMS control flow cannot use this policy yet. Sound objects already swallowed
by a warp have no playable audio time and also reject it; audible objects at a new
warp plateau map to its playable exit.

== Timing focus

kbd:[F3] (or the timer button in the toolbar) switches Compose to *Timing focus*: notes are locked
and every click goes to the timing lanes. Press kbd:[F4] to return to ordinary Compose.

== Finding the tempo

=== Tap tempo

Run *Tap Timing* from the command menu. Start playback and press kbd:[T] (or kbd:[Space]) on every
beat. After four taps an estimate appears; keep tapping until it settles, pick how to round it
(*Round to*), and click *Apply BPM*. Applying changes both the initial BPM and the song's audio
offset to match the tapped downbeat; later BPM changes keep their musical positions. The song needs
a music track, and timing must be unlocked.

=== Beat fitting

With the song's waveform shown in Timing focus, drag a beat line from the narrow rail immediately
left of the timing lanes onto the matching hit in the waveform. The tempo between the previous BPM
change (or anchor) and that beat stretches so the line lands where you drop it; release to commit.

* Hold kbd:[Ctrl] while dragging to snap the BPM to the step chosen with the *BPM snap* buttons
(0.1, 0.25, 0.33, 0.5, 0.75 or 1 BPM); kbd:[Ctrl] + wheel changes the step mid-drag.
* Hold kbd:[Ctrl+Alt] to snap to the onsets Wormedit detects in the audio.
* kbd:[Escape] cancels the fit and leaves timing unchanged.

Fit from the start of the song outward: each fit pivots on the last BPM change or anchor before
it, so earlier fits stay in place.

== The song offset

The toolbar's *Audio offset* (in seconds) moves the whole song against the chart. Its buttons
nudge it by one audio frame or by 10 ms; the lock button stops it from changing by accident.

== Locking timing

kbd:[Ctrl+Shift+T] (*Toggle Timing Lock*) is available in Timing focus (kbd:[F3]) and locks every
timing event: nothing can create, delete, drag, paste or quantize them until you unlock it,
whichever tool or shortcut tries. The lock stays active after returning to Compose with kbd:[F4].
Lock timing as soon as it is right.

[#area-menu]
== The area menu

Drag across empty space with the Select tool to select an area (whole rows), then right-click it.
Besides the timing tools below, the menu quantizes the selection, creates a section, sets the
<<song-preview,song preview>> to the area, loops playback over the area,
and adds the selected sounds to a layer.

== Turning silence into a stop or delay

Select an empty area, right-click it and choose *Convert to Stop* or *Convert to Delay*. The
selected rows become a pause of the same length, and everything after moves earlier so the music
does not change. The *shorten measures* variants also remove those rows from the measures they
were in.

== SV recipes

For osu!mania and StepMania scroll effects, select an area, right-click and choose *Add SV recipe*.
A recipe is a scroll curve over that area, kept as a curve rather than a cloud of SV points:

* *Output*: *SV* (osu!mania SV, StepMania `#SCROLLS`) or *Speeds* (StepMania `#SPEEDS`).
* *Curve*: constant, linear, exponential, bump or a Lua function, with its values. A small plot
above the values shows the whole span as it will be written.
* *Points*: *Count* (evenly spaced), *Grid* (one per line of a snap grid, such as every 1/16) or
*Auto* (added where the curve bends, up to a maximum). Points are only generated when the chart
is exported.

*Add SPEEDS span* does the same for a StepMania speed change across the area.

Recipes appear as areas in the SV (or SPD) lane. Select one to edit it in the inspector. Values
accept decimals or fractions (`0.5`, `1/2`); drag a value up or down to change it, holding Shift
for finer steps. Edits preview in the lane as you make them and apply as one undo step when you
press Enter, leave the field, release a drag or pick an option; Undo reverts them. When values
are rejected the inspector says why and offers *Revert*. The header turns the recipe on or off,
and *More* duplicates, materializes or deletes it. Copy a selected recipe with *Copy*
and place another use with *Paste*. All uses share the same definition: changing its
curve, Lua source, parameters or post-processing updates every use. Each use keeps
its own range and on/off state. Deleting the original use preserves the definition.
Choose *More → Duplicate as independent* to make a separate definition before editing.
Shared recipe Copy/Paste is currently unavailable in scoped BMS charts. Recipes using
a fixed row step require matching chart resolutions; use an independent duplicate
when the grids differ.
When recipes overlap, an *Order* row lists
them; overlapping recipes multiply, and for Speeds the last one sets the transition.

Under *Adjust*, a recipe can carry an ordered post-process pipeline.
*Multiply* scales the batch. *Normalize displacement* (also called *Normalize average*)
preserves the authored baseline's signed integral; *Normalize distance* preserves its
absolute integral. Each accepts a target factor, defaulting to one. *Balance bump* keeps
one region fixed and solves the other against the baseline, including a varying baseline.
Stages run in their listed order on the same sampled step values used by preview and export.
Raw and final samples are transient; the project stores only the recipe and its stages.
Zero denominators, non-finite values and target-forbidden results reject the batch.

=== Impulses

Expand *Impulses* in an SV recipe form to add an exact normalized position `p` and signed
area. The position belongs to `[0,1)`: the interval start is included and its end is excluded.
A standalone impulse can use a constant curve of zero. Coincident impulse areas add.

The recipe stores the impulse, and preview/export compile its contribution into a constant box.
*Box width (ms)* defaults to one millisecond. The requested width must map exactly to the chart
grid and fit within the recipe range. If it is too narrow or cannot be represented, the diagnostic
shows the minimum grid width; choose a representable fraction or increase the chart resolution.
There is no implicit rounding. Warped spans and non-instant SPEEDS transitions are unsupported.

*Compiled impulse boxes* shows entry/exit rows, width, final area and numerical area error.
Multiply and normalization stages scale the structural area along with the continuous curve.
Overlapping recipes multiply their continuous values; impulse contributions add after weighting
by the authored baseline and continuous factors at their entry. Recipes contributing impulses
at the same entry are summed together before weighting by the remaining recipes. No sampled
point cloud is stored in the project.

=== Lua functions

The link:lua-api/index.html[packaged Lua API reference] documents all context fields, return
values, sandbox limits and complete examples. It works offline alongside this manual.

In a Lua-enabled build, choose *Lua function* in the recipe inspector. Use *Choose module…*
to select a `.lua` file within the project, then *Reload* after editing it externally.
The project keeps a frozen source snapshot; reopening or exporting does not reread the file.
Set an entry point when the module returns a table of factories, for example:

[listing]
----
return {
  rise = function(ctx)
    return function(p, ctx)
      return 1 + p * ctx.parameters.height
    end
  end
}
----

Enter `rise` as the entry point and add the exact rational parameter `height`.
The inspector evaluates changes in the background. *Apply* stores a successfully previewed
recipe as one undoable edit; an error keeps the last good graph and reports the Lua location.
*Revert* drops the draft. Evaluation has instruction and memory limits and cannot access files,
the network or the document.

Use *Pure* for functions whose answer depends only on their inputs. They support adaptive sampling
and cannot change captured state or draw random values during a query. Use *Ordered* for counters,
accumulators or seeded randomness. Ordered mode needs fixed sampling and cycle emission counts
whose sum equals the sample schedule, including its endpoint query. Indices start at one and
`p` still spans the whole range. Every new preview or export resets the session from its seed.
Post-processing and composition reuse prepared values without advancing the function.

== Metronome

*Toggle Metronome* (command menu) clicks on every beat during playback. *Timing Settings* sets its
volume and count-in.

[#timing-links]
== Timing links between difficulties

Open *Metadata → Timing links* to choose another chart in the same project as a timing source.
Each category has its own source: BPM, SV/scroll, SPEEDS, Kiai, stops, delays, warps, anchors,
audio offset and measure layout. Scroll and speed recipes follow their output category.

Choose a source and press *Link*. Replacing local data asks for confirmation. A linked category
follows accepted source edits. Editing that category locally creates a *Local override* and
remembers its source; other categories keep following their own sources. *Relink* replaces the
local override with the source's current timing after confirmation. *Detach* keeps the current
timing and forgets the association. Each action can be undone.

The *New chart* button in Project opens the category source choices for the new difficulty.
Leave a category *Independent* for local timing, or choose the chart it should follow. Creating
the difficulty and its selected links is one Undo step. Cancel discards these choices.

Measure links preserve local notes, holds, BGM, BGA, layout segments and beat markers at their
exact musical beats; audio-time markers keep their audio positions. Charts keep their own
measure identities and row resolution. An edit that cannot map exactly, would lose local
content, or needs unsupported retained-source timing changes is rejected for the whole linked
update. Sample anchors require a known, unambiguous project music clock. Detach dependents
before deleting a source chart. Native projects preserve links; interchange exports contain the
resolved timing.
